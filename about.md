# About

I'm **Adithya L**, an undergraduate in **Engineering Design at IIT Madras**.

What interests me most is understanding complex systems well enough to either **predict them, control them, or build computational tools around them**.

That has naturally taken me across several fields that initially look unrelated:

- machine learning,
- fluid mechanics,
- numerical methods,
- nonlinear dynamics,
- control,
- mechanistic interpretability,
- reinforcement learning,
- CAD/CAE,
- and embedded systems.

The common thread is that all of them involve studying how a system transforms information or state.

---

## Physical Systems

For a physical system, I usually think in terms of

\[
\text{state}
\rightarrow
\text{governing dynamics}
\rightarrow
\text{future state}.
\]

Questions then become:

- What equations describe the system?
- Which approximations are valid?
- What quantities dominate its behaviour?
- Is the system stable?
- How sensitive is it to perturbations?
- Can it be reduced without losing important dynamics?
- Can it be controlled?

This is what drew me toward fluid mechanics, dynamical systems, scientific computing, and control.

---

## Learned Systems

A neural network raises surprisingly similar questions.

Given a model state and an input:

- where is information represented?
- how does that representation evolve?
- what computations are performed internally?
- which paths actually affect the output?
- how does optimisation reshape those computations?

This is what interests me about mechanistic interpretability and reinforcement-learning dynamics.

---

## Computation

Between the mathematical model and the final result lies the part I enjoy most: actually making the system computationally tractable.

That can mean:

- discretising differential equations,
- modifying legacy Fortran solvers,
- building GPU training pipelines,
- writing embedded C++,
- connecting meshing and FEA software,
- designing real-time control loops,
- or constructing retrieval and inference infrastructure.

I enjoy moving between theory and implementation rather than treating them as separate activities.
